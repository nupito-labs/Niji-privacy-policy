Niji Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 September 2026

The short version

Niji reads payment notifications on your phone, turns them into a spending record, and keeps that record on your phone. It has no servers. It does not have an account system. Your transaction history is never uploaded, sold, or shared with anyone, including us.

Everything below is detail on that.

What Niji reads

Payment notifications. With your permission, Niji reads notifications posted by the payment and bank apps you select during setup or in Settings → Monitored apps. From those notifications it extracts:

  • the amount
  • who was paid, where the notification says so
  • the time
  • the notification's text, kept alongside the entry so you can check what it was read from

Notifications from every other app on your phone are discarded before their content is examined.

Niji does not read your SMS messages, email content, contacts, photos, call log, or location, and does not request permission for any of them.

What you type. Transactions you add or edit by hand, including any note or payee name you enter.

Text you share into Niji. Niji can be picked from another app's Share menu for plain text — for example, a bank SMS you choose to share manually. That text is parsed on your device to prefill the same add-transaction screen as manual entry, which you review and confirm before anything is saved. It is never read automatically, never sent anywhere, and never stored alongside the saved transaction.

That is the complete list. Niji collects nothing else.

Where it goes

Nowhere. It is written to a database on your device that is encrypted with AES-256 (SQLCipher). The encryption key is generated on your device, stored in the Android Keystore, and never leaves it.

Niji has no server. There is no account to sign up for, nothing to sync, and no analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting service in the app.

The internet permission, stated plainly

Niji's app listing shows the INTERNET permission, and we would rather explain that than have you find it and wonder.

Most of it is Google Play Billing and the Play Integrity API, built into the app and requiring it. Those are used for the one-time purchase and to check the app was installed legitimately. They talk to Google, not to us — we have nothing for them to talk to.

Niji code itself also opens one kind of connection, optional:

  • Currency conversion, only when Online Mode is turned on in Settings, to fetch exchange rates from a public rate provider (api.frankfurter.app). No transaction data is sent — only a currency code and a date.

No part of your spending data is transmitted anywhere, by any means.

Backups, and the one case where data leaves your phone

If you choose to create a backup, Niji writes an encrypted file and asks you where to put it, using Android's standard file picker.

  • The file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a recovery code shown once and never stored. We cannot open your backup. Neither can anyone who obtains the file without that code — and if you lose it, the backup cannot be recovered by us or anyone else.
  • Where the file goes is entirely your choice. If you choose Google Drive or any other cloud location, the file is then held by that provider under their terms and privacy policy, not ours. Niji is not told where you saved it.

This only ever happens because you asked for it.

Who your data is shared with

No one. There are no third-party analytics, advertising, or attribution SDKs in Niji. We do not sell, rent, or share your data, because we never receive it.

The app includes three third-party components, none of which receives your transaction data:

  • SQLCipher — an on-device encryption library. Entirely local.
  • Google Play Billing — processes the one-time purchase. Google receives purchase information under Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Google Play Integrity — verifies the install is legitimate. Also Google's.

Your control over your data

  • See everything: your full history is in the app; Settings → Detection diagnostics also shows what Niji skipped and why.
  • Change or delete any entry: tap a transaction to edit or delete it.
  • Choose what is read: Settings → Monitored apps.
  • Stop it entirely: revoke notification access in Android Settings.
  • Delete everything: uninstall Niji, or use Android Settings → Apps → Niji → Storage → Clear data. Because the data is only on your device, removing the app removes the data. There is no copy for us to delete, and no account for you to close.

Children

Niji is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from anyone. It is a personal finance tool intended for adults.

Indian users (DPDP Act)

Niji processes personal data entirely on your own device and transmits none of it. You retain access, correction, and erasure rights over everything in the app through the controls listed above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above changes with it. Material changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
